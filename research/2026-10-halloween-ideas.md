# Seed Siege: Halloween and seasonal event ideas (October 2026)

Researched 25 October 2026 by the Scout. Halloween is 6 days away. Seed Siege is not live and the Builder is on M1 (the first playable patch).

How to read this: every factual claim has a numbered source, listed at the end. **[official]** means a Roblox Creator Hub or Roblox newsroom page read in this session. **[third party]** means press, a guide site or a fan wiki. **[unverified]** means I could not confirm it from a primary source, or sources disagree. This report does not repeat the market, discovery, DevEx or Ads Manager research in `research/2026-10-game-ideas.md`; it cites that file as "earlier research" where it relies on it.

---

## 0. The short version

1. **Nothing can reach players by 31 October.** Publishing needs M1 to M4 done (saves, shop, phone pass, thumbnail, maturity questionnaire; spec section 9). A Halloween event needs a live game. Diverting the Builder from M1 now would delay launch for a holiday the game will miss anyway. The one honest "in time" action is cheap and internal (section 3a).
2. **What the big games did** follows one shape: a limited-time currency earned by playing, spent in an event shop; content dropped in weekly phases over 3 to 4 weeks; a free reward track or quest line; reskinned enemies or items; and a capstone cosmetic. Grow a Garden ran three weekly "Ghoul Garden" phases from 11 October to 1 November 2025 [3][4][5]; Adopt Me ran weekly drops from 3 October to about 1 to 3 November 2025 [6]; Dress to Impress ran 18 October to 8 November 2025 with a candy currency and a story quest [7].
3. **Roblox's own Halloween event is curated, not open.** 2024 The Haunt (24 October to 1 November) [9], 2025 Halloween Spotlight (23 October to 3 November, more than 30 games) [10], 2026 The Hollow (22 October to 2 November, nine horror games) [11]. I found no public way for a small game to apply. The open tool is **Experience events** on the game's own page, which any owner can create, with a "Notify me" button [13].
4. **Biggest policy risk:** copying the genre's habits. Grow a Garden sold its Halloween egg for 149 Robux with random pets [5] and Toilet Tower Defense used event crates [8]. For Seed Siege that would break the spec's "no paid random items" rule, trigger Roblox's odds and region rules [14], and undercut the plan to reach younger players later. Close second: pressure wording ("LAST CHANCE") that Roblox tells creators to avoid with minors [15].
5. **Top 3 ideas:** (1) "Lolly Night": pests in costumes drop Lollies, spent at Nan's stall on cosmetics only; (2) the Jack-o'-lantern Pumpkin Mortar skin as the free capstone; (3) a Moonlit Patch weekly garden for Halloween week. All three ride on a reusable season framework (section 5) that makes Christmas, Easter and autumn cheap.

**Concrete player numbers.** I found **no reliable concurrent player figure tied to a Halloween event** for any of these games. What exists:

- Grow a Garden's peaks came from non-Halloween updates: 21.3 million on 21 June 2025 and 22.3 million on 23 August 2025 (an "admin war"), and it "doesn't typically sustain its peaks for long" [22] [third party, Wikipedia]. Steal a Brainrot set the record at about 25.8 million on 11 October 2025, during Halloween season but not from a Halloween event [23] [third party, Guinness].
- More than 800,000 players followed Dress to Impress's 2024 Halloween event page before release [24] [third party; follows, not players].
- Roblox says Halloween search traffic "sees a consistent annual spike from September through October", and horror searches "pick up dramatically pre-Halloween" [12] [official, no numbers].
- A Yahoo listicle claims Halloween updates lifted engagement 15% month on month in October 2025; it cites nothing [25] **[unverified]**.

**What drove play and spending (pattern, my reading of the table, not a measured result):**

- **Weekly phases.** Grow a Garden, Adopt Me and TDS split the event into weekly or nightly drops, so players had a reason to come back each week. This fits Roblox's advice that "a weekly cadence for updates is ideal" [15] [official] and Seed Siege's Monday weekly garden.
- **Event currency earned by playing**, spent in a themed shop. Every game above did this.
- **A shared goal** (Grow a Garden's cauldron) turned single play into a community effort.
- **Reskins of existing systems** (pumpkin hat enemies in TDS 2023, Halloween mutations in Grow a Garden) gave a lot of "new" for little work.
- **Spending came mostly from random eggs and crates** in Grow a Garden, TTD and PS99. Seed Siege must not copy this (section 2.3). TDX shows a fairer model: one free track for everyone, with an optional paid skip.

## 2. Roblox platform events and policy limits

### 2.1 Roblox's own Halloween events, and can a small game join?

- **2024, The Haunt:** 24 October to 1 November 2024, hub in Axbury Manor, quests in partner games [9] [third party; game lists were partly leaks].
- **2025, Halloween Spotlight:** 23 October to 3 November 2025, "more than 30 games", runes and keys earned in partner games, exclusive avatar items as rewards; named games include Adopt Me, Dress to Impress and Restaurant Tycoon 3 [10] [official].
- **2026, The Hollow:** started 22 October 2026 and runs to noon PST 2 November, live finale from 31 October, nine horror games (99 Nights in the Forest, Doors, Dandy's World, Piggy, Scary Shawarma Kiosk, Animal Hospital, Demonology, The Mimic, Warden) [11] [third party, GamesBeat, citing Roblox's announcement]. Another guide gives 9 November as the end [26]; I could not read Roblox's own announcement, so the end date is **[unverified]**.
- **Joining:** neither Roblox page explains how games are chosen; the 2025 page speaks of "our creators' most creative, scariest games" [10]. The Hollow is all horror and already running. I found **no application process for small games [unverified]**. Roblox's Creator Events programme is about hosting webinars and streams, not joining platform events [27] [official].
- **What a small game can use:** **Experience events** [13] [official]. The owner of a game can publish up to 10 upcoming or live events; they show in an Events section on the game page with **Notify me**, which sends an inbox notice (and an optional push) when it starts. **Trending Events** needs 1,000 RSVPs. **Off-Platform Featuring** needs the event submitted at least 7 days before it starts, and selection weighs DAU growth, playtime, retention and monetisation. Update announcements (60 characters, one every three days) are a separate tool [13].

### 2.2 16+ discovery and content maturity

- A new game is first shown only to age-checked players 16 and over [16] [official]. Roblox Kids (5 to 8) allows **Minimal or Mild** content; Select (9 to 15) allows Minimal, Mild or Moderate [16].
- **Does spooky content change the questionnaire?** Yes, if it is scary. Fear is "scary or horrifying elements that trigger fear in players"; **Mild** fear includes "jump scares" and "creepy-looking NPCs"; **Moderate** includes "characters with disfigured mouths with realistic blood" [28] [official]. Ghosts and skeletons are not named; they count only if they are creepy [28].
- Violence stays Mild if its consequence is unrealistic, such as "bodies disappearing the moment their health reaches zero" [28]. Seed Siege's pests puffing into leaves fits this.
- **Rule for the pack:** cute, not scary. Pests in costumes (a sheet with eye holes, a cardboard bat wing), soft lighting, smiling pumpkins. **No jump scares, no creepy faces, no sudden loud sounds, no blood or gore.** That keeps fear at nothing or at most Mild "occasional", so the label stays Mild and Kids stays possible later. Change the questionnaire answers only if the content changes them; the owner answers it honestly.

### 2.3 Paid random items

- Any random reward bought with Robux, directly or through a currency bought with Robux (eggs, wheels, crates, luck boosts, re-rolls, keys), must show every outcome and its odds as percentages before purchase [14] [official].
- When `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted` is true for a player, the game must not let them use paid random items and must apply one of Roblox's treatments (a free path, a fixed order, direct purchase at expected value, hide, block or move them) [14]. Regions are not listed on the page [14]; the earlier research notes Brazil bars under-18s.
- **Random rewards earned only by play, with no Robux in the chain, need no disclosure** [14]. So a free daily trick-or-treat roll is fine, as long as Lollies are never sold.
- Event currencies are not named separately, but "in-game currency purchasable with Robux" is covered [14], so **selling Lollies for Robux would pull every random Lolly reward under these rules.** Decision: Lollies are never sold, as Petals are never sold (spec 4.2).

### 2.4 Limited-time pressure

- Roblox: limited items "can create excitement", but "Users must never be misled, confused, or pressured into purchases"; no countdowns that are inaccurate or restart; no false scarcity; "avoid using pushy or urgent messages when interacting with minors", with "LAST CHANCE, ACT NOW" to be replaced by "See Price" and "BUY BEFORE IT'S GONE!" by "Open Shop" [15] [official].
- Roblox's season pass guide also suggests making an event's timer "highly visible to drive a sense of urgency" [29] [official]. These pull in different directions. **Resolution for Seed Siege:** show the real end date plainly ("Lolly Night ends Monday 2 November") on the event card, never on a Robux price, never as a pop up, and never "last chance". This matches the spec's shop rule (5.1: no countdowns in the shop, no pop ups on join).
- **Event-only items:** make every free event reward come back next year (as the "returning" set) or stay earnable later, so missing a week costs nothing permanent. Roblox's own guide supports catch-up mechanics such as double XP in the last week [29].

### 2.5 Is Halloween even big for this audience?

- In Australia, 18% of adults plan to celebrate in 2026 (about 4.4 million), down from 21%; those aged 18 to 34 take part most [30] [third party, ARC and Roy Morgan, 20 October 2026]. Halloween here is a spring holiday.
- Roblox's audience is global and Halloween searches spike every year [12]. The 16+ launch audience overlaps with the age group most into Halloween in Australia [30], though that survey is Australian adults, not Roblox players.

## 3. Timing, told plainly

### 3a. Before 31 October 2026

**No idea in this report can reach players by 31 October.** The game is unpublished and M1 has no saves, shop or tutorial yet (spec section 9). Even a finished event needs a live game, and Roblox's event features need a public game [13].

The only things worth doing in the next six days, if the owner wants anything:

- **Nothing in the build.** Keep the Builder on M1. A Halloween detour now costs launch time and earns zero players.
- **Optional, half a day of Scout or Builder time, no code:** add a `season` field to the data plan so M2 and M3 leave room for the framework in section 5 (a date input to the weekly generator, a cosmetic slot per seed). This is a design note, not a feature.
- **Optional for the owner:** screenshot a pumpkin-lit garden in Studio for a future teaser. Thumbnails and promo images must show real game content [earlier research, thumbnails], so only once the skin exists in game.

### 3b. A spooky season pack ready at launch

Build the ideas in section 4 as data plus a few models, **switched off** until their date window. If launch lands in November or later, the pack waits for Halloween 2027 (ISO week 43 to 44), and most of its assets are reused for the Southern Hemisphere autumn pack below.

**Southern Hemisphere autumn angle.** Australian autumn is March to May, and Easter 2027 is 28 March [31] [third party, matches the Fair Work list]. A "Harvest Moon" autumn pack reuses the moonlit lighting, the Lolly currency renamed to Acorns or Gumnuts, the costumed pests without costumes (leaf piles instead), and an Easter egg hunt (free, fixed eggs hidden on the board, never random paid eggs). Being an Aussie backyard game gives a reason for an autumn event that Northern Hemisphere games lack. I found no data on whether this draws players **[unverified, my idea]**.

### 3c. A reusable season framework

Section 5. This is the real long-term win: each later holiday becomes a data file, a few skins and a checklist.

### 1. Lolly Night (event currency and costumed pests)

- **What:** For the event window, some pests in every run wear costumes: an Aphid in a tiny sheet ("Boo-phid"), a Moth with cardboard bat wings, a Snail with a pumpkin shell. Same stats as the normal pest, so balance and tests are untouched. A costumed pest drops 1 to 3 **Lollies** (Aussie word, not "candy") when it puffs away. Lollies buy cosmetics at **Nan's Lolly Stall** in the Garden Gate: plant hats, a lantern path, a ghost-sheet scarecrow for the shed, a plot sign sticker.
- **Why it entices:** an earned event currency spent in a themed shop is what every game in section 1 did [3][6][7][8]; TDS 2023 used exactly this "enemies in pumpkin hats drop the currency" trick [18]. It rewards normal play, so it lifts play days without a new mode.
- **Effort:** M, about 4 days: costume models or decals on 4 pests (reskin only), drop logic in a new pure `Season` module with Lune tests, a stall screen reusing the shop UI, saving Lollies in the profile (M2 schema bump).
- **Money:** none directly. Lollies are **never sold for Robux** (keeps 2.3 clear) and never buy power.
- **Policy risk:** low. Costumes must read as dress-ups, not scary. Unspent Lollies at the end turn into Petals at a fixed rate, so nothing is lost (fair, and no pressure).

### 2. Jack-o'-lantern Pumpkin Mortar skin

- **What:** A carved, glowing, smiling skin for the existing Pumpkin Mortar seed (spec 3.2), with a little candle flicker when it fires. Earned free: the top Lolly stall item, or the last step of the reward track.
- **Why it entices:** the game already has a pumpkin tower, so this is the most "Halloween" thing per hour of work. A visible capstone reward is what Roblox's own season guide recommends ("a worthwhile final reward to act as a capstone") [29], and Grow a Garden built a Halloween mutation around Jack-o'-lanterns [5].
- **Effort:** S, about 1 day (one model or texture, a cosmetic slot per seed, light effect, reduced motion respected).
- **Money:** free. Shows players that cosmetics exist before any paid skin sets (spec 5.2).
- **Policy risk:** low. Keep the face friendly.
- **By 31 October if live:** the only idea small enough, given as a free login gift with no event around it. Not possible this year because the game is not live.

### 3. Moonlit Patch (Halloween weekly garden)

- **What:** For the Halloween ISO week, the Monday weekly garden uses a fixed spooky theme: night lighting, lanterns, and two special modifiers from the existing list, for example `nightshift` (moles from wave 2, Mint Mist range up) plus `fog` (spec 3.5), with a "Moonlit" ribbon for taking part.
- **Why it entices:** it uses a feature the game already has (the weekly garden and leaderboard, M3), so it gives a whole new themed challenge for very little work. Weekly beats fit Roblox's update advice [15].
- **Effort:** S, about 2 days: a season overlay passed into the weekly generator, night lighting preset, ribbon.
- **Money:** none; everyone plays with the same plants (spec 1).
- **Policy risk:** low. **Design risk:** the weekly generator must stay deterministic and pass Appendix A. Make the overlay an extra input (`WeeklyField.generate(week, overlay)`), with `overlay = nil` giving today's exact results, and test both.

### 4. Free Lolly Trail (event reward track)

- **What:** A 15 step track filled by daily missions ("Clear 5 waves at night", "Shoo 20 costumed pests", "Use a Mint Mist"). Every step free. Ends with the Jack-o'-lantern skin. Double progress in the last 3 days for catch-up.
- **Why it entices:** TTD's Halloween levels 5 and 10 and TDX's 27 level free pass are the tower defence precedent [8][17]; Roblox's guide says daily missions bring players back and recommends double XP at the end for those behind [29].
- **Effort:** M, about 4 days (missions as data, progress saving, a track screen).
- **Money:** none at launch. Later, an optional premium cosmetic track would follow the guide's rule that upgrading mid-season grants earlier tiers [29]; cosmetics only.
- **Policy risk:** medium on wording: show the end date calmly (2.4); missions never need Robux [29].

### 5. Phantom Possum boss night

- **What:** The Night Possum boss (spec 3.3) in a bedsheet with a lantern, as the final wave of a special 10 wave "Haunted Orchard" run built from Night Orchard. Same stats; when beaten it drops a big Lolly haul and runs off giggling.
- **Why it entices:** a named event boss is a clear goal; costumes on existing characters are cheap "new" content (TDS pumpkin hats [18]). **[unverified that it lifts play; my reasoning]**
- **Effort:** M, about 3 days (one costume, a field variant as data, boss intro card).
- **Money:** none.
- **Policy risk:** low, if no jump scare on entry. Possums are native animals; keep the "cheeky visitor, not vermin" tone the spec already uses.

### 6. The Great Pumpkin (community goal)

- **What:** A giant pumpkin in the Garden Gate grows as all players together shoo costumed pests. At set totals everyone gets a cosmetic (a pumpkin hat for the shed). Shown as one bar, no ranking.
- **Why it entices:** Grow a Garden's shared Witch's Cauldron made the event a group effort [4][5]. Good for a small game too: even a few hundred players see the bar move. **[unverified for small games]**
- **Effort:** M to L, about 5 days: a global counter across servers (MemoryStore or DataStore with care for limits), tests, and a fallback if the counter fails (event still works, bar just stops).
- **Money:** none.
- **Policy risk:** low. Set the totals so the last reward is reached even with few players (or scale it to active players), so nobody is punished for a small player base.

### 7. Nan's Halloween garden theme

- **What:** For the event, the garden gets Nan in a witch hat at the Garden Gate, cobweb-free autumn leaves, carved pumpkins on the fence, lanterns, and gentle spooky music (a xylophone tune, not a horror score). Nan's tips get a Halloween line or two (8 words or fewer, spec 2.2).
- **Why it entices:** a seasonal lobby makeover is the cheapest signal that "something is on" (TTD did a Halloween lobby [8]). Matches the owner's open question on "Nan" (spec decision 7).
- **Effort:** M, about 3 days (props, lighting preset, music, tips).
- **Money:** free during the event; later, the same set can be a 149 R$ garden theme pass for all year (spec 5.2), cosmetic only.
- **Policy risk:** low.

### 8. Daily trick-or-treat gate

- **What:** Once a day in the event, knock on Nan's shed door: a free small Lolly gift (fixed 5 Lollies, or a free random 3 to 10). Never a scare.
- **Why it entices:** DTI used a trick-or-treat mechanic [7]; a small daily gift supports the day 2 to 7 return signal discovery weighs (earlier research 2.3).
- **Effort:** S, about 1 day.
- **Money:** none.
- **Policy risk:** low **only if free**: free random rewards need no odds [14]. Do not copy DTI's jumpscare outcome [7], which is Mild fear [28].

### 9. Moonlit plant skin set (paid cosmetic)

- **What:** A glow-in-the-dark recolour for all 10 seeds, sold as a permanent 99 R$ pass (the spec already plans 99 R$ skin sets).
- **Why it entices:** cosmetics are the spec's fair money (5.2); seasonal themes give a reason to buy. No source shows it sells better at Halloween **[unverified]**.
- **Effort:** M, about 3 days (10 recolours, pass wiring from M4).
- **Money:** cosmetic only, sold all year after launch, with no countdown.
- **Policy risk:** low. **Conflict with the spec:** 5.2 puts paid cosmetics in a later update after Kids and Select. This would bring one forward to 16+ launch. Owner's decision; my lean is to keep the spec's order and make the Halloween pack free.

### 10. Spooky Experience event and update notice

- **What:** Create an Experience event "Lolly Night in the Backyard" on the game page with Notify me, and send an update announcement when it starts [13].
- **Why it entices:** it is the one built-in Roblox tool a small game can use, and Notify me puts the event in players' inbox [13]. Off-Platform Featuring needs submission 7 days ahead [13].
- **Effort:** S, half a day of owner time in Creator Hub (art plus text). No code.
- **Money:** none.
- **Policy risk:** low; event art goes through moderation and must show real game content [13], [earlier research, thumbnails].

## 5. The reusable season framework

Goal: every holiday after Halloween costs a data file, some skins and a checklist, not a new system. Fits the spec's style (pure logic in `shared/Logic`, tested by Lune, deterministic from the date).

1. **`Seasons` data module (pure):** a list of seasons, each with an id, a UTC start and end, and parts: `currency` (name, icon, conversion to Petals at the end), `pestCostumes` (pest id to costume id, never stat changes), `seedSkins` (rewards), `weeklyOverlay` (modifier ids and lighting preset for its weeks), `missions`, `shopItems` (Lolly prices), `lobbyTheme`, `tips`. Example entries: Halloween (Lolly Night), Christmas (an Aussie summer Christmas: tinsel on the shed, Santa hats, prawns on the barbie lighting), Lunar New Year, Easter and Harvest Moon (autumn, Acorns or Gumnuts), Winter Frost (June to July in Australia, ties to the existing Frost mode).
2. **`Season.active(nowUtc)` (pure, clock passed in):** returns the season or nil. Lune tests check the boundaries, overlaps (none allowed), and that `nil` leaves every existing test, including Appendix A, unchanged.
3. **Kill switch and date override through Experience configs:** Roblox's `ConfigService` lets the game read a value set in Creator Hub and change it **without publishing a new version** [32] [official]. Use it for `seasonEnabled`, a start or end override, and drop rates, so the owner can stop or extend an event in a minute with 30 minutes a week of time. Default when the config cannot be read: the dates in the data file.
4. **Saving:** one `seasonal` table in the profile: currency balance per season id, owned cosmetics (kept forever), track progress, last trick-or-treat day. Schema version bump with a migration test (spec 6.6). At the season's end, leftover currency turns into Petals at the listed rate.
5. **Fairness rules baked in, tested:** costumes never change stats; season currency is never a developer product; no season item affects the weekly leaderboard; a weekly overlay is the same for everyone; every free reward returns or stays earnable later.
6. **UI reuse:** one event card in the Garden Gate (name, plain end date, "Open stall"), the shop screen reused for the stall, a track screen reused for every season.
7. **Ops checklist per season (owner, about 30 minutes):** Experience event created 7+ days ahead for featuring [13]; update announcement on day 1 [13]; maturity check against 2.2 (any new scary content? no); config kill switch tested; no urgent wording (2.4).

**Effort:** the framework is about M to L, 5 to 6 days, once, ideally right after M4 or in the first post-launch update. Each later season then costs roughly S to M (2 to 4 days of art and data).

---

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not change M1 to M4 for Halloween 2026. Add only a design note now so M2 and M3 leave room for a season input (3a).
2. First post-launch update: the season framework plus ideas 1, 2, 3 and 8 (about 8 days) as one free "Lolly Night" pack, switched on by date. If launch is before late March 2027, reuse it as the Harvest Moon and Easter pack first.
3. Keep every Halloween reward free and cosmetic; Lollies never sold; no random paid anything; cute, not scary.
